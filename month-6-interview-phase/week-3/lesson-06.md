# Deep build — the full FDE exercise: discovery → plan → pitch, end to end

**Week 3 · Deep build · ~3 hrs** · Month 6 — Interview Phase

## Objective
Run the complete FDE simulation in one sitting: fresh situation → discovery (30 min) → plan (90 min) → pitch + hostile Q&A (20 min) — scored as one artefact chain, with a full retro.

## Why this exists
The parts were drilled separately; loops run them chained, tired, and in order. This is the closest rehearsal to the real FDE final round the course can build — and its retro is Week 4's mock-loop targeting data.

## Prerequisites
Week 3 lessons complete.

## Retrieval warm-up (closed-book, 8–12 min)
1. **Previous lesson (Month 6 Week 3, Lesson 5 (Consolidation — narrative mixed gate + applications audit)):** state its objective and its verification in one sentence each — from memory, guide closed.
2. **Spaced review (Month 6 Week 2):** one generative item — write the key command, schema, or rule from that lesson without looking.
3. **Interleaved discrimination:** one item mixing this week's pattern with its most confusable neighbour, unlabelled — name which is which and why.
4. **Prediction:** predict the output/behaviour of today's first build step before you run it. Write the prediction down.
5. Tag every answer with a confidence (0–100%) *before* checking. A miss on a prerequisite for today changes today's plan now, per the opening routine.

## Prerequisite check
Re-run the previous lesson's independent verification (Month 6 Week 3, Lesson 5 (Consolidation — narrative mixed gate + applications audit)) before starting. If it no longer passes, repair first and compress today's objective — never stack new work on a broken base.

## Core concept
Simulation rules: one fresh situation (not Week 3's), no artefact reuse from prior drills except the story bank (real loops let you cite your experience — that's the point of having some), clocks enforced per segment, scoring per segment + a chain score (did the plan answer the discovery's actual findings, or the situation's first paragraph?). The chain score is the FDE-specific measurement: plans that ignore their own discovery are the classic failure.

## Build steps
Expected output is stated *before* you run each step. If you see something else, stop, classify the error (slip / execution-model / API misconception / design misconception), and check the failure notes.

### Step 1 — Discovery segment (30 min)
Per Lesson 2's method on the fresh situation.

**Expected output:** Discovery artefact + restatement committed.

### Step 2 — Plan segment (90 min)
Per Lesson 3's rubric, scoped to *this* discovery's findings.

**Expected output:** Plan artefact committed; an explicit mapping section links plan choices → discovery findings.

### Step 3 — Pitch + hostile Q&A (20 min)
Pitch; ≥3 hostile questions (cost, build-vs-buy, timeline scepticism).

**Expected output:** Q&A record; defences scored for artefact use.

### Step 4 — Chain retro
Score the chain (discovery→plan fidelity, plan→pitch honesty); write the retro with the single biggest chain break.

**Expected output:** Retro committed; the chain break becomes a Week 4 mock emphasis item.

## Independent verification (checkpoint)
A complete, scored FDE simulation exists with a chain-fidelity retro naming your biggest break point.

## Unaided probe (closed-guide)
State your confidence (0–100%) before checking each answer.

1. Teach-back: the simulation's story as a 2-minute narrative — situation, discovery surprise, plan choice, defence — for the bank's flex section.
2. Generative: your chain break + its repair, unaided — then check the retro.
